Product concept · Skin lesion triage

More information between clinical suspicion and the next decision.

MelaninBlind is developing a non-invasive approach to assessing suspicious skin lesions using electrical measurements, with the aim of giving clinicians an additional source of information during assessment.

Designed to support clinical judgement — not replace it
The product

A simple interaction, built around a clinical question.

The proposed experience is designed to be straightforward for a clinician: position a small sensor patch over the lesion, scan it with a compatible phone, and view the measurement on the screen.

01

A small sensor patch is positioned over the suspicious skin lesion.

02

Scan with a phone

View the measurement

The electrical measurement is displayed on the phone for the clinician to consider alongside their examination.

Core product insight

Turn an electrical response into information a clinician can review.

MelaninBlind explores whether measuring how tissue responds to a small electrical signal can provide useful additional information when a lesion is being assessed.

Proposed user outputElectrical measurement

A clear phone-based view for clinical review. Any future risk categories or recommendations would require appropriate clinical validation.

What does it need to do?

Capture an electrical measurement from a selected skin lesion and make that measurement available to the clinician.

What matters most?

A repeatable measurement and a simple, understandable phone-based experience.

What needs testing?

Measurement consistency, placement and contact, repeatability, usability, safety, and performance across a range of lesion types.

What evidence is needed?

Clinical studies must establish whether the measurement adds useful information to clinical assessment and how it should be interpreted.

Important: MelaninBlind is a technology under development. Its diagnostic performance and any effect on biopsy decisions have not yet been established. It is not a substitute for clinical examination, histopathology, or biopsy when indicated.
Our intention

Support the decision. Keep the clinician in control.

Our aim is to explore whether an additional, non-invasive measurement can help clinicians make more informed decisions about suspicious skin lesions — and whether this could eventually reduce avoidable procedures when clinically appropriate, without compromising patient safety.
